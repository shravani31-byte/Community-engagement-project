Everyday choices

Good habits.
Safer online.

Small choices can help protect your privacy, your accounts, and the people around you. Pick a few habits and practise them every day.

01

Pause before you click

Make it a habit Stop when a message creates panic, promises a prize, or asks you to act quickly.

Try this Check the sender and web address independently. Don't use links or QR codes from unexpected messages.

02

Keep personal details private

Make it a habit Protect your address, school, phone number, passwords, live location, and private photos.

Try this Think about who could see a post before sharing. Ask permission before posting someone else's photo.

03

Keep passwords and codes to yourself

Make it a habit Use a different, hard-to-guess password for each account. Never share passwords or one-time passcodes.

Try this Turn on two-step verification with help from a parent or guardian.

04

Check surprising requests

Make it a habit Treat requests for money, gift cards, account access, or secrecy with care.

Try this Contact the person or organization using a number or website you already know is genuine.

05

Be kind and think before posting

Make it a habit Remember that messages and pictures can be copied or shared beyond their original audience.

Try this Don't forward embarrassing posts, rumors, or private conversations. Report bullying and get support.

06

Set boundaries with people online

Make it a habit You don't owe anyone a reply, a photo, your location, or a meeting.

Try this Stop replying if someone pressures or frightens you. Block or report them and tell a trusted adult.

07

Review privacy settings

Make it a habit Check who can see your posts, contact you, tag you, or view your location.

Try this Ask a trusted adult to review the settings with you, especially after an app update.

08

Take a moment away

Make it a habit Make room for sleep, schoolwork, movement, and time with people offline.

Try this Mute notifications while studying or winding down, and notice how online activity makes you feel.

✳

When in doubt, pause and ask. Show a worrying message to a trusted adult. You can ask for help even if you already replied or clicked.
