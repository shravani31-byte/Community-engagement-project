The field guide

Know the signs.
Choose what’s safe.

Scams can look convincing. Use these plain-language guides to slow down, check the details, and know when to ask for help.

01

Spam messages

What it is Unwanted messages sent to lots of people, sometimes to sell something or trick you.

Watch for Unexpected offers, repeated messages, strange senders, or pressure to reply.

Example “Claim your free prize now. Reply YES to continue.”

Protect yourself Don’t reply or follow links. Block or report unwanted messages.

02

Legitimate messages

What it is A genuine message from someone or an organization you recognize.

Watch for Even familiar names can be copied; unexpected requests still need checking.

Example “Practice starts at 4. Meet by the school gate.”

Protect yourself Check the context and verify unusual requests another way.

03

Phishing attacks

What it is A trick to steal information by pretending to be a trusted person or service.

Watch for Urgent threats, login links, spelling oddities, or requests for private information.

Example “Your school account is locked. Sign in here to restore access.”

Protect yourself Don’t use the message link. Visit the official site yourself or ask school staff.

04

Fake links & QR codes

What it is A link or QR code that leads to a fake page or harmful download.

Watch for Misspelled web addresses, shortened links, or QR codes with no clear source.

Example A poster QR code says “Free game coins—scan and sign in.”

Protect yourself Don’t scan unknown codes. Ask where they came from and check the address first.

05

Online fraud

What it is Dishonest activity that tries to take money or valuable information.

Watch for Requests for gift cards, money transfers, fees, or secrecy.

Example “Pay a small delivery fee with a gift card to receive your package.”

Protect yourself Don’t send money. Tell a trusted adult and contact the company independently.

06

Fake jobs & lotteries

What it is False offers of work or prizes used to collect money or personal details.

Watch for A prize you never entered, guaranteed earnings, or a fee to claim a reward.

Example “You’ve been selected for a job. Pay for your starter pack today.”

Protect yourself Verify through official channels. A real prize should not need your password or OTP.

07

OTP & banking scams

What it is A scammer tries to get a one-time passcode or access to a financial account.

Watch for Anyone asking you to read out a code, even someone claiming to be support.

Example “Tell me the six-digit code you just received so I can secure your account.”

Protect yourself Never share codes or banking details. Contact your bank using its official number.

08

Privacy & personal data

What it is Information about you that could identify you or reveal where you are.

Watch for Requests for your address, school, phone number, location, or private photos.

Example A new online friend asks which route you walk home every day.

Protect yourself Keep personal details private. Tell an adult about uncomfortable requests.

09

Password security

What it is Protecting accounts with strong, unique passwords and extra verification.

Watch for Reuse of the same password or messages asking you to send it to someone.

Example “Send me your password and I’ll fix your game account.”

Protect yourself Use a unique passphrase, enable two-step verification, and never share it.

10

Digital footprint

What it is The trail left by what you post, share, and do online.

Watch for Posts showing personal details, live location, or something you wouldn’t want shared widely.

Example Posting a photo with your school badge and today’s location visible.

Protect yourself Pause before posting. Review privacy settings and ask permission before sharing others’ photos.

11

Responsible social media

What it is Using social platforms kindly, thoughtfully, and with respect for others.

Watch for Rumors, pressure to join harmful trends, bullying, or requests to keep unsafe secrets.

Example A group chat urges everyone to repost an embarrassing photo of a classmate.

Protect yourself Don’t amplify harm. Save evidence if needed, report abuse, and speak to a trusted adult.

12

Unwanted contact & pressure

What it is Contact that ignores your boundaries or tries to control, frighten, or isolate you. It can happen to anyone, including girls and young women.

Watch for Requests to keep a chat secret, share your location or private images, meet alone, or do something after you say no.

Example “Keep this between us. Meet me alone after school or I’ll share our chat.”

Protect yourself Don’t meet them or send anything. Stop replying, keep the message as evidence if safe, block or report, and tell a trusted adult. For immediate danger, contact local emergency services.

✳

It’s always okay to ask for help. If a message worries you, stop replying and show it to a trusted adult.
